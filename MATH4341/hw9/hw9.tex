\question[1]{}
Let $\mathcal{U}$ be an arbitrary open cover of $A$. Since $x_n \to
x$, by definition for any open neighborhood $U$ of $x$, $\exists N
\in \mathbb{N}$ such that if $n>N$ then $x_n \in U$; that is to say,
that for all but a finite number of $n$, $x_n \in U$. Take $U$ to be
any $U \in \mathcal{U}$ such that $x\in U$, the points in $A$ not in
$U$ are all sequence elements such that $n \leq N$. Since
$\mathcal{U}$ covers $A$, there must exist at least one $U_n \in
\mathcal{U}$ such that $\forall n \leq N, x_n \in U_n$, since theres
only a finite number of such $x_n$, simply collect all $U_n$ along
with $U$ to obtain a finite subcover of $A$.

\question[2]{}

Let $\{A_i\}$ to be a finite collection of compact subspaces. Let
$\mathcal{U}$ be an open cover of the union of all $A_i$

\[
  \bigcup^N A_i = \bigcup_{U \in\mathcal{U}} U
\]

Consider that, since each $A_i$ is compact, and since $A_i \subset
\bigcup A_i$ implies that $\mathcal{U}$ is an open cover of $A_i$ as
well, for any $i$, we can pick out a finite open subcover $\{U_{ij}\}
\subset \mathcal{U}$ such that
\[
  A_i = \bigcup_j U_{ij}
\]

Since there are only finitely many $A_i$, collecting all such
$\{U_{ij}\}$ would be an open subcover of the union of all $A_i$, that is

\[
  \bigcup A_i = \bigcup_{i,j} U_{ij}
\]

is a finite open subcover, hence, $\bigcup A_i$ is compact.

Since $X$ is Hausdorff, and each $A_i$ is compact, then $\forall i,
A_i$ is closed. The arbitrary intersection of closed sets is in
itself closed, so $\bigcap A_i$ is closed.

Further, since, $\forall j, \bigcap A_i \subset A_j$, $\bigcap A_i$
is a closed subset of a compact space, which is compact.

\question[4]{}

An open set in this topology is given by  $U =
\mathbb{R}\setminus A$ where $A$ is any finite set, including the
empty set. Then any open cover $\mathcal{U}$ of a subspace $X$ is such that
\[
  X  \subset \bigcup \mathbb{R} \setminus A_i = \mathbb{R} \setminus \bigcap A_i
\]

For a collection of finite sets $A_i$. We can construct an open
subcover as follows, take any $A_i$, call it $A_0$, if $X \cap A_0$
is empty, take $X \subset U_1 = \mathbb{R} \setminus A_0$ to be the
finite subcover of $X$. Since $A_0$ is finite, so is $X \cap A_0$.
Now, since $\{\mathbb{R}\setminus A_i\}$ covers $X$, $\forall x \in X
\cap A_0, \exists A_i$ such that
$x\notin A_i$, collect all such $A_i$ for each $x \in X \cap A_0$,
labeled $A_{x}$, then we have the finite collection
\[
  X \subset \mathbb{R} \setminus A_0 \cup \bigcup_{x\in X\cap A_0} \mathbb{R}
  \setminus A_{x}
\]

Thus any set $X$ in this topology is compact.

\question[5]{}

Take $(\mathbb{R}, \mathcal{T})$ where $\mathcal{T}$ is the ray topology.

\[
  \mathcal{T} = \{[a, \infty) \vert a \in \mathbb{R}\}
\]

For any arbitrary $X \subset \mathbb{R}$, any open cover $\mathcal{U}$ such that

\[
  X \subset \bigcup_{U_i \in \mathcal{U}} [a_i, \infty)
\]

Take the $U_i$ with the smallest possible $a_i$, call it $a_m$, then

\[
  X \subset [a_m, \infty) \subset \bigcup_{U_i \in \mathcal{U}} [a_i, \infty)
\]

is clearly a compact. However, take $X = (a,b)$, then $X^c =
(-\infty, a] \cup [b, \infty)$, however, $(-\infty, a]$ is not in the
topology, so $X^c$ is not open, and therefore $X$ is not closed.
